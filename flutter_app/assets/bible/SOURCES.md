# Bible text sources

- Arabic Van Dyck and the old Jesuit Deuterocanonical text, including Psalm 151, are sourced from [St-Takla](https://st-takla.org/Holy-Bible_.html), which identifies the translations and provides vocalized text.
- The Arabic Prayer of Manasseh follows the [Coptic Holy Pascha text at St-Takla](https://st-takla.org/lyrics/ar/liturgy/holy-pascha/483.html). Diacritics and a 15-part division were added for the reader; this liturgical wording is not attributed to the old Jesuit translation.
- The World English Bible Classic with Deuterocanon is from [eBible.org](https://ebible.org/find/details.php?id=eng-web), which dedicates the text to the public domain and retains the World English Bible trademark for faithful copies. The app separates Daniel's Greek additions and displays Greek Esther as a complete book.
- King James additions to Esther are from [scrollmapper Bible Databases](https://github.com/scrollmapper/bible_databases/blob/master/formats/json/KJVA.json), arranged as chapters 10–16.

- Good News Arabic 2025 (`gna`) is supplied by Bible Society in Lebanon via [the Free Use Bible API metadata/source](https://bible.helloao.org/api/arb_gna/complete.simple.json), under [CC BY-NC-ND 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-nd/4.0/). The source covers 66 books. Verse wording is unchanged; the app's JSON layout preserves existing plan keys by regrouping Joel (source 4 chapters → app 3) and Malachi (source 3 → app 4). Source and numbering notices are displayed in the app. Deuterocanonical supplements, Prayer of Manasseh and Psalm 151 remain separate, attributed old Arabic texts and are not represented as GNA wording. See `gna/LICENSE.txt` and `tools/gna_import_report.json`.
- The independent `jesuit` selector exposes only the old Jesuit Deuterocanonical material already stored in `ar_extra`. It is not a complete modern Jesuit Bible and never substitutes Van Dyck text under a Jesuit label. Previously stored Manasseh/Psalm 151 supplements are separately identified.
